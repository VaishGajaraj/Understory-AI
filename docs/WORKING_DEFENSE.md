# Understory — Defense Track

## 1. The capability gap

Adversaries and illicit actors exploit vegetated terrain to conceal logistics routes, staging areas, emplacements, and infrastructure from overhead surveillance. The blind spot is structural, not budgetary: electro-optical satellites cannot see through canopy; commercial SAR constellations (Umbra, ICEYE, Capella) operate at X-band, which scatters off the canopy top rather than penetrating it; and airborne foliage-penetration radar exists but is scarce, sortie-limited, and expensive to task persistently. The result is a standing surveillance gap across precisely the terrain that matters in the Indo-Pacific, Central and South America, and Eastern Europe — dense jungle, forest, and mixed vegetation where concealed activity goes undetected until contact.

NISAR changes the economics of this problem. It is the first free, systematically repeating, global L-band SAR stream ever flown; L-band penetrates canopy, and repeat-pass coherence change detection on the stream reveals surface disturbance beneath the trees — newly cut trails, soil compacted by vehicle traffic, cleared brush, emplaced structures. The data is unclassified, globally shared, and costs nothing to task, because it cannot be tasked: it simply arrives, everywhere, every ~12 days.

## 2. Mission applications

**Counter-narcotics.** The clearest near-term fit. Trafficking infrastructure in jungle terrain — clandestine airstrips, trail networks, riverside loading points, encampments — is built under canopy specifically because overhead assets can't see it. A persistent watch-area alerting capability over corridors of interest gives task forces (the JIATF-S / SOUTHCOM mission set) cueing they currently lack, on an unclassified feed shareable with partner nations, which matters in a mission where most action is executed by host-nation forces.

**Border and partner-nation security.** Detection of new trail cutting and route development in forested border regions, delivered as a capacity-building tool to allied nations — attractive precisely because the data source is free and the product is unclassified, so it can be given away as security cooperation without export-control drama around the data itself.

**Persistent situational awareness in vegetated theaters.** For land components and SOF elements operating in forested terrain: change-monitoring over named areas of interest, with alerts on new ground disturbance — road building, position preparation, logistics activity — between passes. The 12-day revisit makes this an operational-preparation and pattern-development tool, not a tactical tracking one, and it should be sold as exactly that; overpromising cadence is the fastest way to lose a sponsor's trust.

**Monitoring and treaty-adjacent verification.** Documenting infrastructure development in denied or remote areas over months — the persistent, systematic, wide-area nature of the stream suits long-dwell questions ("is activity increasing along this corridor?") better than point queries.

What the capability is *not*: it does not detect people, vehicles in motion, or anything on timescales shorter than the repeat cycle. It detects that the ground was physically disturbed, where, and within which 12-day window. Framing discipline on this point is both operationally honest and ethically load-bearing.

## 3. Why this wins procurement arguments

The solicitation language this maps to — persistent situational awareness in complex terrain, low-cost, attritable or software-only, open architecture, coalition-shareable, integrates with existing tactical systems — recurs across current defense innovation calls. The offer checks every box structurally rather than aspirationally: zero new hardware, zero classified inputs, zero tasking cost, unclassified-executable and partner-shareable by design, and native integration into the tactical ecosystem the user already runs (TAK). Incumbent geospatial analytics firms are oriented around selling their own X-band pixels or broad environmental science products; none has an operational exploitation layer on the NISAR stream. The window to build the definitive labeled disturbance library on a brand-new free national data asset rewards the first mover disproportionately, because the library — not the pipeline — is the accumulating asset.

## 4. Business model outline

**Phase I — feasibility (SBIR Phase I or equivalent, ~$150–300K, 6 months).** Deliver the quantified benchmark: detection precision/recall over instrumented ground-truth sites and externally documented activity areas, minimum detectable event size, detection latency, and a live demonstration of alerts flowing as Cursor-on-Target into a TAK instance. Critically, most of this artifact already exists if the open civilian benchmark has been run — the defense proposal's feasibility evidence is the same table with a different cover page, which is the honest structure of dual-use: one experiment, multiple funders.

**Phase II — operational pilot (SBIR Phase II, ~$1–2M, 12–24 months).** Persistent watch-area alerting for a sponsoring command: standing coverage over sponsor-nominated AOIs, live CoT feeds into the sponsor's TAK environment, analyst feedback loop capturing confirm/reject adjudications into the signature library, and optional automated cueing of commercial X-band collection for confirmation looks at exposed features. The Phase II success metric mirrors the civilian one: detections the sponsor acted on.

**Phase III / production.** Direct contracts or OTA-vehicle production with the transition sponsor; pricing as an annual subscription per watch-area portfolio (a SOC-style monitoring service, not a data license), with tiers by coverage area and alert SLA. The commercial (compliance/MRV) revenue stream funds development between government contract cycles rather than substituting for them — the standard and credible dual-use financing story.

**Customer development sequence.** SBIR topics are the on-ramp but not the strategy; the strategy is a named transition sponsor. Candidate first conversations: JIATF-S / SOUTHCOM science-and-technology advisors (counter-narcotics is the least crowded, most partner-shareable mission), INDOPACOM land-component innovation cells, and SOF acquisition channels (SOFWERX-style events take cold pitches with working demos). The pre-solicitation conversation with a technical point of contact — showing the benchmark before the topic drops — is where Phase I awards are actually won.

**Moat.** Same as the civilian track, stated in defense terms: the biome-indexed, analyst-adjudicated disturbance signature library compounds with every deployment; the pipeline is replicable (and deliberately open), the library is not. Additionally defensible in this market: accumulated past-performance and the sponsor relationships themselves, which in defense contracting function as a moat in their own right.

**Honest risks.** NISAR is a civilian science mission — no tasking authority, no guarantees of continuity, and any conflict-driven data-access restriction is outside anyone's control (mitigation: the library and methods transfer to ROSE-L, NISAR follow-ons, and any future L-band source, including commercial L-band if it materializes). The 12-day cadence bounds the mission set to persistent surveillance, not time-sensitive targeting. And the same benchmark risk as the civilian track: if precision over external ground truth is poor, there is no product — which is why the benchmark precedes every funding conversation.

## 5. Technical specification (defense-specific deltas)

The core pipeline is identical to the open civilian stack — NISAR L2 interferometric products from ASF, coherence time-series stacks, seasonal baseline models of expected natural decorrelation, persistence/clustering/geometry filtering, ML classification trained on the labeled library. This section specifies only what differs for defense delivery.

**Output format and integration.** Primary deliverable is Cursor-on-Target (CoT) event messages carrying: event polygon or centroid, disturbance class, confidence score, first/last pass timestamps, and a link to the evidence package. Delivery into the sponsor's TAK Server over standard TLS-mutual-auth federation; compatibility with ATAK/WinTAK end-user devices comes free with CoT compliance. Evidence package per alert (coherence time-series chart, anomaly raster, pass metadata, methodology version) renders as a static HTML/PDF artifact retrievable by URL from the alert — the operator-facing equivalent of the compliance evidence pack.

**Watch-area management.** Sponsor defines AOIs as polygons with priority tiers; the system maintains standing coherence stacks per AOI, processes each new pass within hours of product availability (NISAR operational latency is 36–72 hours from acquisition; end-to-end alert latency is therefore pass-plus-roughly-three-days, and should be quoted as such), and emits only above-threshold, persistence-confirmed events. Threshold profiles are per-AOI: a counter-narcotics corridor tolerates a different false-alarm rate than a broad-area watch.

**Analyst loop.** A thin review console (the same component as the civilian design) where the sponsor's analysts adjudicate alerts; every confirm/reject writes to the signature library under the sponsor's data-rights agreement. Data-rights structure matters and should be negotiated deliberately: the government gets unlimited rights in the delivered alerts and reports; the contractor retains rights in the underlying library and models (standard SBIR data-rights protections make this the default, which is a structural reason to enter through SBIR rather than a services contract).

**X-band cueing.** Optional automated tasking of commercial high-resolution X-band (Umbra/ICEYE/Capella APIs) for confirmation looks at exposed features associated with an alert — clearings, road segments, structures. This is a broker function: the system never owns pixels, it spends the sponsor's imagery budget only on coherence-qualified locations, which is the cost-efficiency argument in one sentence.

**Deployment and security posture.** The processing pipeline runs on commercial cloud against public data and remains unclassified end-to-end; deployment options in order of friction are contractor-hosted SaaS feeding the sponsor's TAK server (fastest), GovCloud deployment (IL4/IL5 if the sponsor requires alerts be handled as CUI), and air-gapped delivery of alert products only (no live feed) for the most restrictive environments. Nothing in the baseline architecture requires classified inputs; if a sponsor later fuses alerts with classified sources, that fusion happens on the sponsor's side of the boundary. This unclassified-by-design posture is a feature, not a limitation — it is what makes the product partner-shareable and demo-able.

**Hardening deltas from the civilian stack.** Deterministic, versioned processing (every alert reproducible from archived inputs and a pinned methodology version — the defense analog of scientific reproducibility, and a requirement the moment alerts inform operations); audit logging of the full alert lifecycle; and geographic access controls so that watch-area definitions, which are themselves sensitive, are compartmented per sponsor.

## 6. Relationship to the open project

The open civilian project and the defense track share the pipeline, the benchmark, and the physics; they must not share a brand, a repository identity, or a partner-facing narrative. The open project's credibility with NGOs, indigenous monitoring programs, and the scientific community depends on it being what it says it is — a public-good forest-monitoring effort — and the defense business's credibility with sponsors does not require, and is not helped by, entangling those communities. Practically: separate entity or DBA for the defense work, separate communications, the open repo referenced as prior art in proposals (a strength: program offices favor open architectures with proprietary model weights), and a firm internal rule that the labeled contributions of civilian partners are governed by the terms they were contributed under — field-verified labels from an Amazon NGO partnership serve the open dataset those partners signed up for, and equivalent defense-side libraries are built from sponsor adjudications under sponsor agreements. One pipeline, two libraries, two brands, no crossed wires.
